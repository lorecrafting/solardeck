Third audit, six new dimensions the first two never covered: **cost truth, energy truth, regulatory,

## B1 — The BOM says $383.30. The BOM sums to $400.72. (blocker)

`docs/BOM_COST_ESTIMATE.md` carries a headline **$383.30** and a category table whose rows actually
sum to **$400.72**:

| Row | Stated |
| :--- | ---: |
| Subtotal | $338.30 |
| Assembly / test / yield | $21.37 |
| Tooling & NRE (amortised) | $16.94 |
| Contingency @ 6% | $20.29 |
| Scrap / rework | $4.22 |
| **Sum** | **$400.72** |

The delta is **$19.02**, explained by nothing in the document. Worse, it propagates: `spec_values.json`
carries `bom_total_high: 383.3`, `README.md` quotes it, and `docs/STATUS.md` is generated from it. The
existing Phase 10 number checker validates `text == spec_values` — so it certified $383.30 as correct
against a JSON that was itself wrong. **Two errors that agree look like one truth.** This is the single
most important structural lesson of all three audits: a consistency checker without a ground-truth
derivation is a tautology with extra steps.

Fix: derive the total from the rows in code (Phase 14), then reconcile headline to $400.72 or delete
$19.02 of rows with a stated reason.

## B2 — The solar energy budget is 3–4× optimistic, and its assumptions are undisclosed (blocker)

**Generation.** Base V1 harvest = bezel strip (0.15 W peak) + 15 W fold-out hiking panel = 15.15 W.
At a Hawaii-typical **4.28 kWh/m²/day** and a **70%** combined panel/MPPT/angle derate, that is
≈ **45 Wh/day**. The docs assume **150 Wh/day**.

Getting 150 Wh/day out of 15.15 W needs a **9.9 h insolation day at ~100% efficiency**. The best
recorded day on earth is ≈7.2 h. **Unreachable.**

**Consumption.** Unpacking the quoted "3–4 days off-grid" at the quoted idle and exploration loads:
- Idle-only, 2 W: 54 Wh/day ⇒ the assumed 150 Wh/day surplus is **~3× idle**.
- Exploration duty, 12 h active at 4.5 W + idle: ≈ **57 Wh/day**, against **45 Wh/day** generated.
- On the **89.6 Wh 2-cell pack** that is **1.6 days**; on the CSV **7.6 Wh** cell it is **18–23 h**.

So "3–4 days off-grid" is true only at idle; and the CSV cell must not inherit the endurance claim —
it is a claim/verb mismatch that only appears when the numbers are divided.

**Also unstated:** solar **generation is not routed into device power**. The TPS25750 PD path and the
TPS61088 (for SMA connectors) push harvested energy into the pack, not the rails. At peak the Pi needs
2.2 A on 5 V while remaining budget is 1.7–1.8 A — the claim and the architecture differ. Either state
"harvest charges the pack; the pack feeds the device" or rework the path.

Fix: a **power budget table with every assumption named** (insolation hours, derate, duty cycle, panel
orientation), generated from `spec_values`, plus a Phase 14 check that
`generated_wh >= consumption_wh × days − pack_wh` actually balances.

## D3 — No regulatory or certification dimension exists anywhere (blocker for shipping)

There is no RF certification matrix, no safety-file list, no battery transport document, no import
classification. For a device with **cellular, Wi-Fi/BT, LoRa, and a user-swappable LiFePO4 pack** this is
the difference between a prototype and a product:

| Domain | Needed | Missing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cellular | PTCRB / GCF, carrier approval (EG25-G is pre-certified — must cite the module cert) | ✗ |
| Wi-Fi/BT | Module cert reuse (CM4 has it) vs intentional-radiator rules for the antenna | ✗ |
| LoRa / sub-GHz | FCC Part 15.247/15.231 + EU EN 300 220, per-region EIRP tables | ✗ |
| EMC / safety | FCC Part 15B, EN 55032, EN 62368-1 | ✗ |
| RF exposure | SAR/MPE assessment for the modem antenna 8 mm from the hand | ✗ |
| Battery | UN 38.3 (transport), IEC 62133-2, EU 2023/1542 battery passport | ✗ |
| Materials | RoHS/REACH, WEEE registration, Prop 65 | ✗ |

Add `docs/REGULATORY.md` with an owner and a target date per row, and make "no cert = no ship" explicit.

## D4 — The third audit's other findings

1. **Battery service hatch is unopenable by design.** §7.6 says "rear service hatch (2× M2.5)", but §4
   also specifies "adhesive-backed rear" in the same document, and gasket compression requires even
   pressure that a 2-screw hatch cannot provide. One must win.
2. **Module heights vs internal height.** CM4 module 1.5 mm + carrier standoffs 4 mm + heatsink
   4.8 mm = 10.3 mm inside a 12.5 mm cavity; the connector itself consumes 3.5 mm. Only ~2 mm remains
   for the eMMC-side shield and cell wrap. Not impossible, but the stack was never actually summed.
3. **Salt-fog requirement collides with the exposed pogo bus.** IP67 + 96 h ASTM B117 + bare pogo
   contacts implies gold-over-nickel thicknesses and a bezel wipe spec that do not exist. Either the
   rear bus is sealed (drop the claim) or specify Au 0.8 µm / Ni 2 µm.
4. **Ergonomics dimension missing entirely.** 232 × 170 × 14 mm and 620 g is heavier than the devices
   it competes with (reMarkable 403 g, Boox Tab Ultra 480 g). The palm shelf carries 130 mm of unsupported
   mass. A 620 g two-hand device is fine; a 620 g *one-hand* device is not. The specs never say which.
5. **No field-return loop.** No RMA/repair-path definition, no spare-parts P/N list published, no
   serviceability SLAs. For a 30-year claim, the repair path *is* the product.
6. **`firmware/` has no defined interface contract.** RP2350 talks to CM4 over SPI + 2 GPIO, but no
   register map exists, so firmware and userland cannot be developed independently or verified apart.
7. **No thermal model.** 620 g, sealed, 14 mm thick, 4.5 W peak. An all-sealed aluminium slab is a heatsink,
   but the enclosure spec has no thermal resistance number, no junction-temperature budget, and no
   specification of whether the cell sits in the heat path (calendar-fade lives there).

## 7. What the three audits together say

| Audit | Question | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Is the documentation true? | No — 10 blockers, now fixed |
| 2 | Will it survive 30 years? | Materials layer, no — 6 gaps, now specified |
| 3 | Does it add up, and can it ship? | **No — the money doesn't add up and there is no certification path** |

The pattern is consistent and diagnostic: every audit finds that the *documentation is internally
coherent and externally unverified*. Coherence is the thing this repo is best at; it is also the thing
that most easily disguises a wrong assumption, because a checker written against the same assumption
agrees with it.

The rule this earns: **every number needs one derivation, not two agreements.** Table sums, energy
budgets, and booleans like "is it certified" all belong in code that recomputes from primitives, never
in a second prose location.
